# Market Research: Selling "AI Business Audits" to US HVAC Business Owners
*Prepared 2026-09-08. Client acquisition channel: organic short-form video (TikTok, IG Reels, YouTube Shorts, FB Reels). Sources cited inline.*

## 1. WHO — Demographics of US HVAC Business Owners

### Market size
- **~117,000–120,000 HVAC contractor businesses** in the US; industry revenue ~**$156B/yr** (IBISWorld 2025 via search summary). Broader company databases list up to 475k entities when including sole props/inactive listings.
  - https://www.ibisworld.com/industry-statistics/number-of-businesses/hvac-service-franchises-united-states/
  - https://withorbital.com/data/tam-reports/hvac/
  - https://companydata.com/usa/hvac-companies-usa/
- Roughly **1.6 nonemployer (solo) businesses for every employer establishment** — the market is dominated by tiny shops.
  - https://startbusinessbystate.com/hvac-industry-statistics/

### Business size & revenue bands
- Majority are **small, family-owned, owner-operated, <5 employees, <$1M revenue** (often 1–5 trucks).
- Average **nonemployer** HVAC business grossed **~$78k/yr** (2023). Small shops with a few techs: **$100k–$500k**; mid-size: up to **$2M**; multi-location: $2M+.
- Average employer establishment: **~10.9 employees, ~$805k annual payroll**.
- Owner take-home: median ~$76k; typical range **$70k–$150k**; averages skewed up by big shops.
  - https://www.workyard.com/construction-management/hvac-facts-statistics
  - https://www.servicetitan.com/blog/hvac-owner-salary
  - https://www.housecallpro.com/resources/hvac-business-owner-salary/

**Practical segmentation for the audit offer:** the sweet spot is the **$500k–$3M shop with 2–10 techs** — big enough to feel the pain (missed calls, dispatch, admin) and pay a retainer, small enough to have no ops staff or CTO.

### Age / gender / background
- Trades businesses skew old: **~41% of small businesses run by owners 55+**; trade businesses (HVAC/plumbing) are "typically 15+ years old with an owner in their 50s–70s" — the "silver tsunami" of retiring boomer owners is a live theme (also a buyer wave of PE and millennial acquirers entering).
  - https://www.teamshares.com/resources/silver-tsunami/
  - https://www.business2community.com/small-business/millennial-business-acquisition-trades-hvac-plumbing/
- Workforce is **>90% male; women ~9%** of HVAC workforce; women-owned HVAC firms are a small but growing niche (Women in HVACR org exists).
  - https://hvac-blog.acca.org/taking-advantage-of-being-a-woman-owned-or-woman-managed-hvac-business/
- Most owners are **former techs who "got good at the tools" and started a company** — strong technical identity, weak/reluctant on sales, marketing, and office systems. (Consistent theme across podcasts/forums; not a census stat.)

### Buying roles (who actually says yes)
- **Owner-operator** (1–5 trucks): decision-maker, also the guy answering the phone in the truck. Fast decisions, price-sensitive, hates recurring costs he doesn't understand.
- **Owner + spouse/office manager**: extremely common structure. The **spouse/office manager runs phones, scheduling, invoicing, reviews — she is often the real evaluator** of anything touching calls/CSR workflow. Content that speaks to "your office manager is drowning" lands with both.
- **GM / Ops manager** (10+ tech shops): exists at ~$3M+; more formal evaluation, already courted by ServiceTitan/Avoca-tier vendors.

## 2. WHERE (Geographic)

### Highest concentration of HVAC businesses
- **California (~12.3k), Florida (~9.8k), Texas (~9.1k)** have the most HVAC businesses; **TX, CA, FL, NY, PA together = ~36% of all US HVAC locations** (counts vary by source/definition; CA and TX trade #1 depending on dataset).
  - https://www.statista.com/statistics/1243016/plumbing-and-hvac-contractors-by-state-usa/
  - https://withorbital.com/data/tam-reports/hvac/
- Employment: **FL (~37.4k workers), CA (~35.6k), TX (~31.9k)** lead. Top metros by HVAC workers: **NY–Newark–Jersey City (~22.8k), Dallas–Fort Worth (~10.1k), Miami–Ft. Lauderdale–West Palm (~9.5k)**.
  - https://www.hvacclasses.org/blog/top-cities-hvac-workers
- **Sun Belt confirmed as demand core**: Phoenix called "one of the largest and fastest-growing home-service markets in the US" (AC season April–October; 13 new HVAC locations opened in metro Phoenix in one recent 90-day window). DFW is the 4th-largest residential HVAC metro. Houston, Tampa, Atlanta, Las Vegas similar profile.
  - https://www.withorbital.com/data/hvac/phoenix-az/fastest-growing/
  - https://www.builtontenth.com/hvac-research/ac-repair-market-dallas-tx
- **Targeting note:** Sun Belt owners have longer peak seasons (more missed-call pain, more after-hours emergencies) and heavier competition (more need for reviews/marketing edge). Northern markets have sharper dual peaks (heat in winter, AC in summer) and deeper shoulder-season cash crunches.

### Seasonality (critical to pitch timing)
- Peaks: **summer cooling (May–Aug)** and **winter heating**; **spring + fall are "shoulder seasons"** where call volume drops **30–40%** while overhead stays flat.
- **40% of HVAC companies cite cash flow management as their top challenge**; shoulder season is described as "not a slow month — a liquidity crisis."
  - https://www.usehatchapp.com/blog/hvac-shoulder-season-tips
  - https://stephsbooks.com/blog/hvac-seasonal-cash-flow
  - https://www.whytecpapc.com/post/the-hvac-contractors-guide-to-surviving-slow-seasons-cash-flow-strategies-that-actually-work
- **Implication:** owners have *time to talk* in shoulder seasons (Mar–May, Sep–Nov) and *maximum pain awareness* right after peak (Aug–Sep: "we missed hundreds of calls in July"). September–November is arguably the ideal window to sell an audit ("fix this before next summer"). During peak they're too slammed to take meetings — but peak-season content ("you're missing $1,200 calls right now") hits hardest emotionally.

## 4. PAIN POINTS (with real numbers & language for hooks)

### Missed calls / after-hours (the #1 wedge)
- Home-service businesses miss **~27% of inbound calls** on average (ranges cited 27–74% in peak season); **each missed HVAC call ≈ $500–$1,200+ in lost revenue** (figures vary by source: $300–$800 same-day jobs, ~$847/day average loss cited).
- **85% of callers won't leave a voicemail or call back** — they dial the next contractor.
- Even answered calls: one vendor claims **62% are mishandled** (wrong info, missed urgency, poor booking).
  - https://www.cloudtalk.io/blog/best-hvac-ai-virtual-receptionist/
  - https://aireceptionisthomeservices.com/
  - https://www.getnextphone.com/blog/best-virtual-receptionist-for-hvac
- Language owners use: "the phone doesn't stop ringing in July," "I'm on a roof and can't answer," "my wife answers the phones," "we were slammed and let calls go to voicemail."

### Can't hire techs
- Chronic, industry-defining complaint. Real forum quote: *"People, we struggle to find skilled technicians and installers to perform the services we provide. This is seriously hindering our growth and a major concern."* Average tech age reported as high as 55; shortage threads run 8+ pages on HVAC-Talk.
  - https://contractingbusiness.com/rant/solving-hvac-industrys-biggest-problem
  - https://hvac-talk.com/vbb/threads/2232571-Commercial-HVAC-Tech-Shortage/page8
- **Audit angle:** "You can't hire another tech — but AI can give the ones you have 20% more billable hours."

### Unsold estimates / no follow-up (biggest hidden money)
- Typical residential HVAC initial close rates: **23–30%** (healthy = 40–60%; top quartile 62%+) — i.e., **~70% of quoted work walks**.
- Most companies **follow up once or never**; 80% of deals take 8–12 touches.
- SMS follow-up gets **45–55% response** vs 20–30% for calls; automated sequences convert at ~2.8× one-off attempts; a 10-point close-rate lift is worth **~$28k/month** for a mid-size shop (~$336k/yr).
  - https://www.usehatchapp.com/blog/hvac-estimate-follow-up-response-rates
  - https://onepath.ai/blog/unsold-estimate-management
  - https://www.builtontenth.com/diagnostic-tools/estimate-followup-calculator
- Hook math: "You quoted $80k last month and closed $24k. Nobody ever called the other $56k back."

### Cash flow / seasonality
- **40% of HVAC companies name cash flow their top challenge**; shoulder-season volume drops 30–40% with flat overhead. Maintenance-agreement enrollment (the classic fix) is exactly the kind of follow-up/automation play an audit can systematize.
  - https://www.usehatchapp.com/blog/hvac-shoulder-season-tips
  - https://www.trusspayments.com/blog-posts/actionable-strategies-for-managing-cash-flow-during-hvac-off-seasons

### Admin overload / office chaos
- Owner or spouse doing scheduling, invoicing, payroll, permits at 9pm; "wearing all the hats." Dispatch described as whiteboards, group texts, double-booked techs. (Probook exists precisely to automate dispatching.)
  - https://contractortoolstack.com/software/avoca-ai/

### Reviews / marketing
- **91% of homeowners rate online reviews important when choosing an HVAC contractor; 56% go to Google first.** 68% of consumers require 4+ stars; ~half won't consider a business with <20 reviews; recency matters.
  - https://www.achrnews.com/articles/155206-91-of-homeowners-rely-on-online-reviews-before-picking-contractors
  - https://guaranteedremovals.com/google-review-statistics-for-the-home-services-and-contractors-industry/
- Owners also gripe about **lead costs** (Angi/HomeAdvisor shared leads, Google LSA costs) and marketing agencies that "take $2k/mo and send nothing."

### Phrase bank for hooks (owner vernacular)
"We were slammed" · "shoulder season" · "turnover rate" (tune-up→replacement) · "trucks" as unit of size ("we run 6 trucks") · "CSR" (the office person) · "flat rate vs time & materials" · "maintenance agreements / club members" · "the big guys" (PE-backed consolidators) · "parts changers" (insult) · "can't find good help" · "the phone is my leash" · "my wife does the books" · "Angi leads are garbage."

## 5. CONTENT — What Works in Short-Form for This Audience

### Formats with evidence behind them
1. **Cost-of-inaction math ("one thing costing you $X")** — leverages the $1,200/missed call and $28k/mo unsold-estimate stats above. Specific dollar figures out-perform generic "AI will change your business" claims.
2. **Screen-recording / live demo of AI answering an actual HVAC call** — "show, don't tell." Every AI-receptionist vendor leads with call-recording demos because they convert skeptics (e.g., Jobber's AI Receptionist demos, "This AI Receptionist Has Changed The Home Services Game" on YouTube). A 30-sec clip of an AI booking a no-cool call at 11pm is the single strongest asset.
   - https://www.getjobber.com/features/ai-receptionist/
   - https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=8byzd51cdF0
3. **Before/after case study numbers** — blue-collar content marketing guidance is unanimous: project case studies, before/after, jobsite video, "teach first" content earns trust; testimonials from other contractors are the strongest social proof.
   - https://www.clearvoice.com/resources/how-blue-collar-businesses-create-impactful-content/
   - https://percepture.com/construction-insights/content-marketing-for-construction/
4. **POV / skit / "day in the life" parody** — the top viral comedy formats (day-in-the-life parody, expectation vs reality, POV skits, fake tutorial) map perfectly onto contractor life ("POV: it's 7pm and you're still doing invoices in the truck"). Hook in first 1–2 seconds + relatable situation + twist.
   - https://flowshorts.app/blog/ideas-for-a-funny-video
   - https://www.plannthat.com/short-video-ideas-small-businesses/
5. **Myth-busting / anti-hype** — "AI won't replace your CSR, here's what it actually does," "3 AI tools that are a scam for contractors, 2 that aren't." Positions the consultant as the honest broker in a hype-saturated niche.
6. **Whiteboard / calculator content** — walk the math of a 27% miss rate on their call volume. Wrench Grid and Built on Tenth publish "unsold estimate recovery calculators" — a video version of that calculator is a proven lead magnet format.
   - https://wrenchgrid.com/calculators/unsold-estimate-recovery-calculator/

### Trust rules for this skeptical audience
- **Authenticity is mandatory**: "it's not just an occupation, it's a lifestyle" — content that "tries too hard" or smells like agency-speak gets torched in comments. Use trade language correctly (trucks, CSR, tune-ups, no-cool calls, shoulder season) or lose the room instantly.
   - https://www.clearvoice.com/resources/how-blue-collar-businesses-create-impactful-content/
- **Proof > promises**: real call recordings, real dashboards, real dollar recoveries, named (or at least described) client shops.
- **No hype**: this audience has been burned by SEO agencies, lead sellers, and coaching programs. "I'm not selling you software, I'm finding where you're leaking money" is the right frame.
- **Existing proof this positioning works**: contractor-facing AI vendors (Avoca, Rilla) grew largely through podcast appearances and owner-community word of mouth, not ads — e.g., Avoca's "We Booked 400 Calls a Week" episode on Owned and Operated. Guesting on Service Business Mastery / To The Point is a parallel organic channel to shorts.
   - https://www.ownedandoperated.com/post/owned-and-operated-173-we-booked-400-calls-a-week-how-avoca-ai-is-shaping-home-services

## 6. COMPETITION & OFFER BENCHMARKS

### What AI audits actually sell for (2026 market)
- **Basic AI audit (8–12 hrs): $2,500–$5,000. Comprehensive (15–30 hrs): $5,000–$15,000.** Low-end productized: $397 flat (async, written roadmap) up to $1,500 fixed (Treetop); $2,500 (HiVergent, 1–2 weeks, report+roadmap); $3,000 (Salt Technologies: opportunity map, feasibility, ROI); $4,500 fixed (MadXR: workflow inventory, impact/effort-ranked opportunity map, security review, ROI-based roadmap).
- Standard deliverables: **current-state assessment, ROI estimates per use case, 30/60/90-day implementation roadmap**.
- AI consultants generally: $150–$350/hr; $5k–$25k projects.
  - https://www.consultkit.ai/blog/how-to-price-an-ai-audit-for-a-small-business-with-real-numbers-1774170452753
  - https://ariesconsultinggroup.com/blog/ai-readiness-audit-cost/

  - https://aiessentials.us/blog/how-much-does-it-cost-to-hire-an-ai-consultant-for-my-small

### The dominant agency business model (direct competition)
- **Free audit → setup fee → monthly retainer** is the standard AI-automation-agency (AAA/GoHighLevel) play: **$1,500–$3,000 setup + $500–$1,500/mo retainer** per local-business client. "Book a free automation audit" is the standard CTA (Think AI Works, Autoesta, etc.).
- Typical stack sold: **AI receptionist, missed-call text-back, database reactivation, Google review engine** — usually white-labeled GoHighLevel.
  - https://thinkaiworks.com/gohighlevel-automation-agency/
  - https://autoesta.com/gohighlevel-ai-agents-guide/

  - https://www.myvoiceaiconnect.com/blog/sell-ai-receptionist-to-hvac-companies

### Positioning gap for the solo consultant
- Vendors sell **one tool**; AAA agencies sell **one GHL snapshot**. Almost nobody sells a **vendor-neutral, whole-business bottleneck diagnosis** ("where are YOU leaking money: phones, quotes, follow-up, dispatch, reviews, admin — ranked by dollars") for the 2–10 truck shop. That's the audit's differentiation, and it justifies charging for the audit itself instead of giving it away as a thin sales doc.
- **Pricing implication:** a paid audit at **$1,500–$3,500** (below "consultant" scary pricing, above free-audit spam) + implementation at **$2k–$5k setup / $750–$1,500/mo** sits inside proven market bands. Anchor against the pain numbers: one recovered install (~$10–15k ticket) pays for everything.
- Common typical AI use cases to propose in audits (all validated above): (1) AI receptionist / after-hours answering, (2) missed-call text-back, (3) estimate follow-up SMS sequences, (4) review request automation, (5) maintenance-agreement/database reactivation campaigns for shoulder season, (6) dispatch optimization (bigger shops), (7) back-office paperwork automation.

## Caveats / verification to-dos
- Facebook group member counts aren't crawlable — verify from inside FB before citing in content (Hook Agency's list at https://hookagency.com/blog/best-hvac-facebook-groups/ is the best directory; site blocked from this research environment).
- Business-count figures vary by source (IBISWorld ~117k contractors vs Census/state datasets); use "roughly 120,000 HVAC contracting businesses" publicly.
