**The gap she can own:** nobody in Broward offers *on-site, hands-on* operations work for the 2-15 truck shop at a price that shop can pay. National networks want $8K-$36K/yr plus travel and a shop big enough to delegate; fractional COOs start at $3K-$6K/month and are virtual; software consultants stop at the software; marketing agencies stop at the lead. Two things no one else bundles: Florida 489.119 advertising compliance and CareerSource-reimbursed team training.

**Compliance hook, F.S. 489.119(5)(b):** "The registration or certification number of each contractor shall appear in each offer of services, business proposal, bid, contract, or advertisement, regardless of medium, as defined by board rule." Vehicles bearing a contractor's name/artwork must show the number conspicuously; stationery and novelties are exempt. Penalty: the board **shall issue a notice of noncompliance** for a first offense and **may fine or cite** if not corrected within **30 days** or on any repeat. Rule 61G4-12.011 extends "advertisement" to websites, email and social posts **[secondary source]**; Rule 61G4-23.001 lists this as a minor violation; Rule 61G4-19.001 sets the citation at **$100** plus investigation costs. Serious or repeat matters go through DBPR's Uniform Complaint (form DBPR 0070). Sources: flsenate.gov/laws/statutes/2024/489.119; law.cornell.edu 61G4-19.001 and 61G4-23.001.

## 4. How to price and position a first engagement (5-25 employee HVAC shop, Broward)

**Positioning line:** "The local operator who actually builds it." Against Nexstar/BDR/CertainPath she is cheaper, in-person and implementation-first; against software consultants she owns the whole system (CRM + QuickBooks + people); against agencies she fixes the leak before they pour in leads. The 489.119 scan is the free door-opener: most 2-15 truck shops have a website, Google Business Profile or truck wrap missing the CAC number.

**Recommended ladder (benchmark view; the playbook adopts a lighter first-3-clients version)**

1. **Free License & Listing Scan (15 min):** website, GBP, social, trucks checked against 489.119 + review-profile snapshot. Produces a one-page "fix list" and a reason to meet.
2. **Operations Audit, $3,500 fixed (range $2,500-$4,500), 2-3 weeks:** on-site day, ride-along, QuickBooks and CRM review, written 90-day plan. 100% credited toward step 3.
3. **Systems Build Sprint, $6,500 (range $4,500-$9,000) for Housecall Pro/Jobber + QuickBooks; $9,000-$15,000 when ServiceTitan is involved; 4-6 weeks:** pricebook, dispatch rules, QuickBooks sync, automations, SOPs, plus **a scoped team-training block invoiced separately at a per-participant rate** so the employer can apply for CareerSource reimbursement on that line.
4. **Fractional Ops Retainer, $3,000/mo (range $2,500-$4,500), 3-month minimum, ~15-20 on-site hours.** Caps her at 4-5 concurrent clients.
5. **Strategic Planning Day, $4,000 incl. prep and 90-day scorecard**, offered after a retainer quarter.
6. **Website + Reputation Makeover, $3,000-$5,000 rebuild + $300/mo review automation**, or referred to a partner agency for a fee.

**Why this order:** a 5-25 employee shop in Broward does roughly $750K-$4M; $3,500 is under 0.5% of revenue and is the smallest check that still buys a decision-maker's attention. Only the training line is realistically grant-eligible (consulting and implementation are not "direct training costs" under IWT), so keep training visibly separate, keep each training engagement under the $10,000 elevated-approval threshold, and never promise reimbursement until the employer has an executed CareerSource contract.
